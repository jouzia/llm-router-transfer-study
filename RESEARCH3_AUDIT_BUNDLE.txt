# Evaluating Cross-Benchmark Transfer of a Lightweight LLM Router

**Research type:** Empirical evaluation and reproducibility study  
**Status:** Experimental results available; broader generalization and algorithmic novelty not established

## Overview

Large language model (LLM) routing selects a model for a given prompt from a pool of candidate models. A router may use prompt characteristics to make this decision, with the goal of balancing response quality, model capability, and inference cost.

This project investigates whether a lightweight router built from lexical prompt features can transfer across benchmark families, and how its performance compares with fixed-model baselines.

The study evaluates a Ridge-based prompt-feature router on source benchmark data and examines transfer to GPQA and MMLU-Pro. It also investigates how the choice of fixed-model baseline changes the interpretation of routing performance.

The central focus is **empirical transfer, baseline selection, and evaluation reliability**, rather than claiming a novel routing algorithm.

## Research Question

How reliably does a lightweight lexical-feature router transfer across benchmark families, and which evaluation choices explain its performance gap relative to fixed-model baselines?

### Research objectives

- Evaluate a lexical-feature Ridge router on held-out benchmark prompts.
- Compare transfer performance against a strong fixed-model baseline.
- Examine the sensitivity of comparisons to source-validation baseline selection.
- Quantify uncertainty using paired differences and bootstrap confidence intervals.
- Assess whether the available evidence supports claims of reliable transfer or generalization.

## Methodology

### Router

The experimental router uses lexical features extracted from prompts and a Ridge-based prediction approach to select a model from a candidate pool.

The router is evaluated against fixed-model baselines. The experiments distinguish comparisons against a globally selected model, such as Gemini 2.5 Pro, from comparisons against a model selected using source-validation data.

### Evaluation protocol

The experiments include:

1. **Source-data audit:** Inspect benchmark coverage, model pools, record schemas, score distributions, and metric consistency.
2. **Initial transfer evaluation:** Evaluate on GPQA and MMLU-Pro target prompts.
3. **Baseline analysis:** Compare the router with a fixed global baseline and a validation-selected baseline.
4. **Repeated source-validation splits:** Repeat baseline selection to investigate the sensitivity of results to the source-validation split.
5. **Provenance and prior-work review:** Audit data provenance, licensing caveats, related work, and the strength of potential research claims.

Repeated source-validation splits reuse the same target prompts. They therefore measure sensitivity to the selection procedure and should not be interpreted as independent replications across target datasets.

## Experimental Results

The following results summarize the completed transfer experiments.

| Experiment | Router accuracy | Baseline accuracy | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GPQA transfer | 81.82% | 84.85% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) | -3.03 percentage points |
| MMLU-Pro transfer | 83.87% | 85.67% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) | -1.80 percentage points |
| MMLU-Pro, validation-selected baseline | 83.87% | 79.47% (Qwen3 Thinking) | +4.40 percentage points |

### GPQA

The router achieved a mean score of 0.8182, compared with 0.8485 for Gemini 2.5 Pro.

- Paired difference: -3.03 percentage points.
- 95% bootstrap confidence interval: -7.07 to +1.01 percentage points.
- Interpretation: The experiment does not establish superiority over the fixed Gemini baseline.

### MMLU-Pro

The router achieved a mean score of 0.8387, compared with 0.8567 for Gemini 2.5 Pro.

- Paired difference: -1.80 percentage points.
- 95% bootstrap confidence interval: -2.50 to -1.13 percentage points.
- Interpretation: The router underperformed Gemini 2.5 Pro on this evaluation.

Against the validation-selected Qwen3 Thinking baseline, the router scored 4.40 percentage points higher, with a reported 95% confidence interval of +3.3 to +5.5 percentage points.

This result illustrates why baseline-selection protocols matter: the conclusion changes depending on which fixed model is used for comparison.

### Repeated baseline-selection experiments

Across ten source-validation splits on the same target prompts:

- **MMLU-Pro:** The mean difference against the selected baseline was +0.35 percentage points, with a range from -1.83 to +4.40 points.
- **GPQA:** The mean difference against the selected baseline was +1.21 percentage points, with a range from -3.03 to +16.16 points.
- Gemini 2.5 Pro was selected as the baseline in six of the ten splits.

The results demonstrate sensitivity to baseline selection. Because the target prompts are reused across splits, these figures do not establish independent cross-dataset replication.

## Main Findings

1. **Transfer performance depends on the comparison baseline.** A router may outperform a source-validation-selected model in a particular experiment while underperforming a stronger fixed baseline.
2. **The router did not outperform Gemini 2.5 Pro on either reported target benchmark.** The observed differences were -3.03 percentage points on GPQA and -1.80 percentage points on MMLU-Pro.
3. **Baseline selection is unstable across source-validation splits.** The selected fixed model varies, affecting the measured routing advantage.
4. **Broad generalization remains unestablished.** The completed experiments do not demonstrate reliable superiority across independent target datasets.
5. **Algorithmic novelty has not been established.** The current evidence supports an empirical evaluation of transfer behavior and baseline sensitivity, not a claim of a new state-of-the-art routing method.

# Evaluating Cross-Benchmark Transfer of a Lightweight LLM Router

**Author:** Shaik Jouzia Afreen H.

**Repository:** [https://github.com/jouzia/llm-router-transfer-study](https://github.com/jouzia/llm-router-transfer-study)

## Abstract

Large language model (LLM) routers select a model for each input, aiming to exploit complementary model strengths. A router that performs well on source tasks may not retain its advantage on a different benchmark. This study evaluates a lightweight prompt-feature router trained using records from three ArenaHard domains—coding, creative writing, and mathematics—and tests transfer to GPQA and MMLU-Pro. The source extraction audit reports 750 prompts, 33 models, 11 prompt features, and no detected prompt-text, ground-truth, record-availability, or score-alignment inconsistencies. In the current reported evaluation, the router obtains a mean score of 0.8182 on 198 GPQA prompts, compared with 0.8485 for a fixed Gemini 2.5 Pro baseline (difference −3.03 percentage points; reported prompt-bootstrap 95% confidence interval [−7.07, +1.01] percentage points). On 3,000 MMLU-Pro test prompts, the router obtains a mean score of 0.8387, compared with 0.8567 for Gemini 2.5 Pro (difference −1.80 percentage points; reported interval [−2.50, −1.13] percentage points). A source-validation-selected baseline can be weaker: on MMLU-Pro, the router exceeds the selected Qwen3 Thinking baseline by 4.40 percentage points, but this does not establish superiority over the stronger fixed Gemini baseline. Repeated source-validation splits reuse the same target prompts and are sensitivity analyses, not independent replications. The current evidence supports a cautious empirical transfer study, but not a claim that the router outperforms the fixed Gemini baseline or establishes a novel routing algorithm. The score semantics, candidate-model eligibility rules, and correspondence between generated results and committed code must be confirmed before submission.

**Keywords:** large language models; model routing; cross-benchmark transfer; generalization; evaluation methodology; reproducibility

## 1. Introduction

LLM routing assigns each query to a model from a candidate pool. Its motivation is that different models may have complementary strengths, while routing may improve response quality, cost, or latency relative to always using one model. However, source-domain performance does not guarantee transfer to a held-out benchmark, and conclusions can change depending on the fixed-model baseline.

This study asks: **How well does a lightweight prompt-feature router trained on ArenaHard coding, creative-writing, and mathematics records transfer to GPQA and MMLU-Pro?** The aim is evaluation and transfer analysis, not a claim of a new state-of-the-art routing method.

Prior routing benchmarks make unified evaluation and simple baselines important. LLMRouterBench reports a benchmark and unified framework for evaluating LLM routing across many datasets and models [1]. RouterBench provides a framework for evaluating multi-LLM routing systems [2]. RouteLLM investigates routing using preference data and provides a related approach for comparing model-routing strategies [3]. Together, this prior work motivates explicit transfer protocols, transparent candidate pools, and strong baseline comparisons.

### Research questions

- **RQ1:** What is the router's performance on held-out GPQA and MMLU-Pro compared with a fixed strong model?
- **RQ2:** How sensitive are results to the source-validation seed, regularization selection, and fixed-model baseline selected using source validation?
- **RQ3:** Do the current experiments support reliable transfer or superiority over a strong fixed model?

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Prompt-feature router

The router extracts 11 features from each prompt:

1. Character count.
2. Word count.
3. Line count.
4. Digit count.
5. Question-mark count.
6. Exclamation-mark count.
7. Code-symbol count.
8. Code-fence presence.
9. URL presence.
10. Equation-symbol presence.
11. Question-word presence.

Word counts use a regular-expression tokenizer. Binary indicators capture the presence of code fences, URL patterns, equation symbols, and selected question words. The implementation defines code symbols using occurrences of `{}`, `[]`, parentheses, semicolons, and angle brackets. Equation-symbol presence is detected through a regular expression covering selected arithmetic and equation-related symbols. The question-word indicator checks for selected terms, including “what,” “why,” “how,” “when,” “where,” “which,” “who,” “explain,” and “compare,” without regard to letter case.

The implementation trains a separate pipeline for each candidate model, comprising `StandardScaler` followed by Ridge regression. Each regressor predicts the candidate model's benchmark-provided score from the prompt features. The router selects the candidate with the highest predicted score.

The regularization parameter is selected from the candidate set `{0.1, 1, 10, 100}` using equal-weight average validation utility across the three source domains: ArenaHard coding, creative writing, and mathematics. The primary configuration uses seed 2026 and a validation fraction of 20% within each source domain. If validation utilities tie, the implementation prefers the larger alpha. After selecting alpha, the final per-model regressors are refitted using the complete source feature-and-score table.

Target scores are not used for source fitting or for selecting the regularization parameter in the documented source-only protocol. The final artifact audit should confirm that this separation holds for each reported experiment and that target outcomes were used only for evaluation.

The model-selection operation uses `argmax` over the predicted scores. In an exact prediction tie, this operation selects the first candidate in the model ordering supplied to it. The final implementation record should preserve the candidate ordering because it can affect tie resolution.

### 3.2 Baselines

Two comparisons must remain separate.

1. **Fixed strong baseline:** Gemini 2.5 Pro, used as a stable reference across target evaluations.
2. **Source-validation-selected baseline:** a candidate model selected using source-validation data only. Its identity can change with the validation split and it can be weaker than Gemini 2.5 Pro.

The selected-baseline comparison is informative about whether the router improves on a model chosen by the source-validation procedure. It is not interchangeable with the fixed-strong-baseline comparison. A gain against a validation-selected baseline does not establish that the router beats the strongest fixed model.

For reproducibility, each experiment should report the candidate pool, baseline-selection rule, selected baseline, validation utility, and seed. Any comparison across different candidate pools should be explicitly identified rather than treated as a like-for-like experiment.

### 3.3 Evaluation and uncertainty

Performance differences are computed as the router's prompt-level score minus the comparison baseline's score on the same target prompts. Positive differences favor the router; negative differences favor the baseline.

Confidence intervals are reported as percentile intervals obtained by resampling target prompts with replacement and calculating the mean paired difference for each bootstrap sample. The primary Day 4 evaluation uses 10,000 resamples with random seed 2026. The repeated source-validation analyses in Days 7 and 8 use 2,000 bootstrap resamples per comparison, with bootstrap seeds derived from each validation seed by adding 50,000.

Positive paired differences count as router wins, negative differences as losses, and exact zero differences as ties. Candidate selection by `argmax` is distinct from these post-evaluation win/tie/loss counts: the former selects a model based on predictions, whereas the latter summarizes observed paired score differences.

These intervals quantify uncertainty from resampling the observed target prompts under the evaluated setup. They do not capture variability across new target datasets, benchmark versions, model releases, or changes in candidate eligibility. Repeated source-validation seeds reuse the same target prompts and therefore represent sensitivity analyses rather than independent replications.

The final submission should verify that the interval endpoints in the manuscript exactly match the archived result files and the scripts that generated them. Bootstrap intervals should not be interpreted as evidence of broad transfer beyond the evaluated prompts.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Held-out transfer against Gemini 2.5 Pro

The current reported results are summarized below. Values are presented as mean scores pending final confirmation of metric semantics.

| Target | Prompts | Router mean score | Gemini 2.5 Pro mean score | Difference (router − Gemini) | Reported 95% interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GPQA | 198 | 0.8182 | 0.8485 | −0.0303 | [−0.0707, +0.0101] |

For GPQA, the router is 3.03 percentage points below Gemini 2.5 Pro. The reported interval includes zero, so this result does not establish a statistically clear difference under the stated prompt-bootstrap procedure.

For MMLU-Pro, the router is 1.80 percentage points below Gemini 2.5 Pro. The reported interval is entirely negative, indicating lower router scores on the evaluated prompts under this procedure.

Taken together, these results do not support a claim of router superiority over the fixed Gemini baseline on either target benchmark.

This is not evidence that the router beats the strongest fixed model. Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 0.8567 on the same target prompts, above both the router and the selected Qwen3 Thinking baseline.

The result illustrates why baseline-selection protocols matter. A router can exceed a source-validation-selected model while still underperforming a strong fixed reference. The two comparisons answer different research questions and must be reported separately.

## 5. Discussion

The current evidence does not show that the router improves on Gemini 2.5 Pro in either target benchmark. It underperforms by 3.03 percentage points on GPQA and 1.80 percentage points on MMLU-Pro. The reported GPQA interval includes zero, while the reported MMLU-Pro interval is entirely negative.

The selected-baseline results illustrate why baseline policy matters. A router may exceed a model chosen by source validation while still underperforming a strong fixed reference. Both comparisons are useful, but they answer different questions. The fixed baseline assesses the router against a stable reference; the selected baseline assesses the effect of a particular source-validation selection procedure.

The repeated-split analyses further demonstrate that the selected baseline is sensitive to the validation split. The observed range of router-minus-selected-baseline differences is wide, especially on GPQA. Since the target prompts are held constant across these runs, this variation should be interpreted as sensitivity to the source-validation and baseline-selection procedure rather than independent evidence of transfer across new datasets.

The defensible contribution at this stage is a structured empirical evaluation of cross-benchmark transfer and baseline sensitivity for a lightweight prompt-feature router on two target benchmarks. The results do not establish algorithmic novelty, robust generalization across a broad benchmark suite, cost savings, or latency benefits. Cost and latency claims should remain excluded until their metric definitions, units, and comparability have been independently validated.

The study is also limited by the candidate pool, the available recorded model outputs, and the benchmark-specific scoring conventions. Any broader claim requires additional target datasets, transparent candidate eligibility, and comparisons against relevant published routing methods under a common evaluation protocol.

## 6. Limitations

1. **Limited target coverage:** Only GPQA and one MMLU-Pro test split are reported. The findings do not establish transfer to other benchmark families or deployment settings.

2. **Baseline dependence:** Conclusions differ between Gemini 2.5 Pro and a source-validation-selected model. A positive difference against a weaker selected baseline should not be treated as superiority over a strong fixed model.

3. **Repeated target reuse:** The ten source-validation seeds share the same target prompts. They are sensitivity analyses, not independent replications across target datasets.

4. **Candidate-pool differences:** The source extraction reports 33 models, whereas the MMLU-Pro configurations report 13 candidates. The final release must document the exact candidate intersection and eligibility rule for each experiment.

5. **Uncertainty scope:** Prompt-bootstrap intervals condition on the observed target prompts and do not quantify dataset-level transfer uncertainty, future model changes, or benchmark-version variation.

6. **Metric provenance:** Reported values are described as mean scores until the source records and evaluation scripts confirm the metric semantics. Cost and latency are excluded until definitions, units, and comparability are validated.

7. **Implementation transparency:** The router uses 11 prompt-level features, standardized Ridge regression, source-domain validation to select regularization, and maximum predicted score to select a candidate model. The primary configuration uses seed 2026, 20% validation data per source domain, and alpha candidates of 0.1, 1, 10, and 100. Reproducibility depends on retaining the corresponding scripts, feature-extraction code, candidate-model lists, configuration files, and compatible benchmark records.

8. **Result provenance:** The manuscript's reported numbers must be cross-checked against the exact generated result files and their generating scripts. The existence of a current script does not, by itself, prove that it generated an earlier result unless the script version or hash was recorded at experiment time.

9. **Novelty not established:** A complete related-work review and direct comparisons with relevant published baselines are required before making a novelty claim. This study should be positioned as an empirical transfer and evaluation study unless stronger evidence is obtained.

10. **Licensing and redistribution:** The source archive and underlying dataset/provider terms require final verification before redistribution. Raw archives or restricted records should not be included in the public repository unless the applicable permissions are clear.

## 7. Conclusion

The current experiments evaluate a lightweight prompt-feature router trained on ArenaHard coding, creative-writing, and mathematics records and tested on GPQA and MMLU-Pro. The reported results do not establish superiority over the fixed Gemini 2.5 Pro baseline. The router scores below Gemini on both GPQA and MMLU-Pro, with the reported MMLU-Pro interval entirely negative and the GPQA interval including zero.

Apparent gains against a source-validation-selected baseline depend on which baseline is selected and must be interpreted separately. Repeated source-validation runs reveal sensitivity to baseline selection, but reuse the same target prompts and do not constitute independent replications.

The appropriate conclusion is a cautious empirical study of transfer and evaluation methodology, not a state-of-the-art or novel-algorithm claim. Before submission, the reported scores, confidence intervals, candidate pools, score semantics, and provenance documentation should be reconciled with the committed experiment artifacts.

    "arenahard_coding",
    "arenahard_creative_writing",
    "arenahard_math",
}

def feature_row(text):
    text = text or ""
    words = re.findall(r"\b[\w']+\b", text)
    lines = text.splitlines()

    return {
        "char_count": len(text),
        "word_count": len(words),
        "line_count": len(lines),
        "digit_count": sum(c.isdigit() for c in text),
        "question_mark_count": text.count("?"),
        "exclamation_count": text.count("!"),
        "code_symbol_count": sum(text.count(c) for c in "{}[]();<>"),
        "contains_code_fence": int("```" in text),
        "contains_url": int(bool(re.search(r"https?://|www\.", text))),
        "contains_equation_symbol": int(bool(re.search(r"[=+\-*/^]", text))),
        "contains_question_word": int(bool(re.search(
            r"\b(what|why|how|when|where|which|who|explain|compare)\b",
            text, re.I
        ))),
    }

for p in (ARCHIVE, META, MATRIX):
    if not p.is_file():

with META.open(newline="", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f:

    (r["dataset"], str(r["index"])): r
    for r in matrix
}
selected = [
    r for r in metadata

    and r.get("split") == "test"

]

        except Exception as exc:
            archive_errors.append(f"{member_name}: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")
            continue

        model_names_by_domain[dataset].add(model)

        for rec in records:
            idx = str(rec.get("index", ""))
            if not idx:
                archive_errors.append(

            key = (dataset, idx)
            origin = rec.get("origin_query")
            prompt = rec.get("prompt")
            text = origin if isinstance(origin, str) and origin.strip() else prompt

            if not isinstance(text, str) or not text.strip():
                archive_errors.append(

                )
                continue

            item = records_by_domain[dataset].setdefault(
                idx, {"texts": {}, "scores": {}, "ground_truths": {}}
            )
            item["texts"][model] = text
            item["scores"][model] = rec.get("score")
            if "ground_truth" in rec:
                item["ground_truths"][model] = rec.get("ground_truth")

            matrix_row = matrix_by_key.get(key)
            if matrix_row is None:
                score_checks.append(

                )
            elif model not in matrix_row:
                score_checks.append(
                    f"Model column missing: {dataset}, {model}"
                )
            else:
                try:
                    archived_score = float(rec["score"])
                    matrix_score = float(matrix_row[model])
                    if abs(archived_score - matrix_score) > 1e-9:
                        score_checks.append(

                        )
                except (KeyError, TypeError, ValueError):
                    score_checks.append(
                        f"Invalid score: {dataset}, {model}, index={idx}"
                    )

ground_truth_mismatches = []

for dataset in sorted(DOMAINS):
    expected_models = model_names_by_domain[dataset]

    for idx, item in sorted(
        records_by_domain[dataset].items(),
        key=lambda kv: int(kv[0]) if kv[0].isdigit() else kv[0]
    ):
        texts = item["texts"]
        if len(texts) != len(expected_models):
            missing_model_records.append(
                f"{dataset}, index={idx}: "
                f"{len(texts)}/{len(expected_models)} model prompts"

        distinct_texts = set(texts.values())
        if len(distinct_texts) != 1:
            prompt_mismatches.append(

        truths = [
            json.dumps(v, sort_keys=True, ensure_ascii=False)
            for v in item["ground_truths"].values()

            ground_truth_mismatches.append(
                f"{dataset}, index={idx}: ground truth differs across models"

        # Do not silently select one version when model prompts disagree.
        # Use only verified-consistent prompt text for feature generation.
        if len(distinct_texts) != 1:
            continue

        text = next(iter(distinct_texts))
        features = feature_row(text)
        row = {
            "dataset": dataset,
            "index": idx,
            "prompt_text_source": "origin_query when available, otherwise prompt",
            **features,
        }

        matrix_row = matrix_by_key.get((dataset, idx))
        if matrix_row is None:
            continue

        for model in sorted(expected_models):
            if model not in matrix_row or model not in item["scores"]:
                row[model] = ""
            else:
                row[model] = matrix_row[model]

        rows_out.append(row)

import ast
import csv
import json
import re
import tarfile
from collections import defaultdict
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
from sklearn.linear_model import Ridge
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler

ROOT = Path(__file__).resolve().parent.parent
AUDIT = ROOT / "day1_audit"
ARCHIVE = ROOT / "bench-release.tar.gz"
META = AUDIT / "all_result_metadata.csv"
SOURCE = AUDIT / "prompt_feature_score_table.csv"
OUT = AUDIT / "DAY4_GPQA_TRANSFER_RESULTS.txt"
DETAIL = AUDIT / "DAY4_GPQA_PER_PROMPT_RESULTS.csv"
CONFIG = AUDIT / "DAY4_GPQA_ROUTER_CONFIG.txt"

FEATURES = [
    "char_count", "word_count", "line_count", "digit_count",
    "question_mark_count", "exclamation_count", "code_symbol_count",
    "contains_code_fence", "contains_url", "contains_equation_symbol",
    "contains_question_word",
]
ALPHAS = [0.1, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0]
SEED = 2026
BOOTSTRAPS = 10000

# Load only the feature function from the existing script, without
# executing its archive-extraction workflow.
source_code = (ROOT / "extract_prompt_features.py").read_text(encoding="utf-8-sig")
tree = ast.parse(source_code)
feature_def = next(
    node for node in tree.body
    if isinstance(node, (ast.FunctionDef, ast.AsyncFunctionDef))
    and node.name == "feature_row"
)
isolated = ast.Module(
    body=[ast.Import(names=[ast.alias(name="re")]), feature_def],
    type_ignores=[],
)
ast.fix_missing_locations(isolated)
exec(compile(isolated, "existing_feature_row", "exec"), globals())

def fail(message):
    raise SystemExit("STOP: " + message)

# Read the three source domains from the existing feature/score table.
with SOURCE.open(encoding="utf-8-sig", newline="") as f:
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(f))

if not rows:
    fail(f"Source feature table is empty: {SOURCE}")

source_domains = sorted({r["dataset"] for r in rows})
expected_domains = {
    "arenahard_coding",
    "arenahard_creative_writing",
    "arenahard_math",
}
if set(source_domains) != expected_domains:
    fail(f"Unexpected source domains: {source_domains}")

meta_columns = {"dataset", "index", "prompt_text_source", *FEATURES}
model_names = sorted(set(rows[0]) - meta_columns)
if len(model_names) != 33:
    fail(f"Expected 33 source candidate models, found {len(model_names)}")

X = np.array(
    [[float(r[c]) for c in FEATURES] for r in rows],
    dtype=float,
)
Y = np.array(
    [[float(r[m]) for m in model_names] for r in rows],
    dtype=float,
)
domains = np.array([r["dataset"] for r in rows])

if not np.isfinite(X).all() or not np.isfinite(Y).all():
    fail("Source table contains missing or non-finite feature/score values.")

# Hold out 20% of each source domain for alpha selection.
rng = np.random.default_rng(SEED)
train_indices, val_indices = [], []
for domain in source_domains:
    indices = np.flatnonzero(domains == domain)
    if len(indices) < 5:
        fail(f"Too few source prompts in {domain}: {len(indices)}")
    indices = rng.permutation(indices)
    n_val = max(1, int(round(0.20 * len(indices))))
    val_indices.extend(indices[:n_val])
    train_indices.extend(indices[n_val:])

train_indices = np.array(train_indices, dtype=int)
val_indices = np.array(val_indices, dtype=int)

# Select alpha by equal-weight average routing utility across source domains.
alpha_results = []
for alpha in ALPHAS:
    predictors = []
    for j in range(len(model_names)):
        model = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), Ridge(alpha=alpha))
        model.fit(X[train_indices], Y[train_indices, j])
        predictors.append(model)

    predictions = np.column_stack([
        model.predict(X[val_indices]) for model in predictors
    ])
    selected = predictions.argmax(axis=1)
    actual = Y[val_indices, :][np.arange(len(val_indices)), selected]

    per_domain = {
        d: float(np.mean(actual[domains[val_indices] == d]))
        for d in source_domains
    }
    alpha_results.append((float(np.mean(list(per_domain.values()))), alpha, per_domain))

# Select highest validation utility; on ties prefer stronger regularization.
best_utility, best_alpha, best_domain_utility = max(
    alpha_results,
    key=lambda item: (item[0], item[1]),
)
# Choose global baseline by equal-weight average model performance
# across the three source domains. No GPQA scores are used here.
global_means = {}
for model in model_names:
    domain_means = []
    for domain in source_domains:
        domain_rows = [r for r in rows if r["dataset"] == domain]
        domain_means.append(
            float(np.mean([float(r[model]) for r in domain_rows]))
        )
    global_means[model] = float(np.mean(domain_means))
global_model = max(model_names, key=lambda m: (global_means[m], m))

# Fit the frozen router on all source prompts.
final_models = []
for j in range(len(model_names)):
    model = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), Ridge(alpha=best_alpha))
    model.fit(X, Y[:, j])
    final_models.append(model)

# Save source-only configuration before opening GPQA result files.
with CONFIG.open("w", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    f.write("DAY 4 — SOURCE-ONLY ROUTER CONFIGURATION\n")
    f.write(f"Source rows: {len(rows)}\n")
    f.write(f"Source domains: {', '.join(source_domains)}\n")
    f.write(f"Candidate models: {len(model_names)}\n")
    f.write(f"Alpha candidates: {ALPHAS}\n")
    f.write(f"Selected alpha: {best_alpha}\n")
    f.write(f"Global baseline model: {global_model}\n")
    f.write(f"Global baseline source macro-mean: {global_means[global_model]:.8f}\n")
    f.write("GPQA scores were not read during source fitting or selection.\n")
    f.write("\nValidation utility by alpha:\n")
    for utility, alpha, per_domain in alpha_results:
        f.write(f"alpha={alpha}: macro_utility={utility:.8f}; {per_domain}\n")

# Load GPQA metadata and the exact candidate model files.
with META.open(encoding="utf-8-sig", newline="") as f:
    metadata = list(csv.DictReader(f))

gpqa_meta = [
    r for r in metadata
    if "gpqa" in r["dataset"].lower()
    and r["model"] in model_names
]
if len(gpqa_meta) != 33:
    fail(f"Expected 33 GPQA files for the shared candidate pool; found {len(gpqa_meta)}")

if len({r["model"] for r in gpqa_meta}) != 33:
    fail("GPQA metadata has duplicate or missing candidate model entries.")

gpqa_records = defaultdict(dict)
gpqa_texts = defaultdict(dict)
archive_errors = []

with tarfile.open(ARCHIVE, "r:gz") as tar:
    members = {
        m.name.lstrip("./"): m for m in tar.getmembers() if m.isfile()
    }
    for meta in gpqa_meta:
        member_name = meta["archive_path"].lstrip("./")
        member = members.get(member_name)
        if member is None:
            archive_errors.append(f"Missing archive member: {member_name}")
            continue
        try:
            handle = tar.extractfile(member)
            if handle is None:
                raise ValueError("Could not read archive member")
            payload = json.load(handle)
            records = payload.get("records", [])
            if not isinstance(records, list):
                raise ValueError("records is not a list")
            for rec in records:
                idx = str(rec.get("index", ""))
                origin = rec.get("origin_query")
                prompt = rec.get("prompt")
                text_value = (
                    origin if isinstance(origin, str) and origin.strip()
                    else prompt
                )
                if not idx or not isinstance(text_value, str) or not text_value.strip():
                    raise ValueError(f"Missing index/prompt in {member_name}")
                score = rec.get("score")
                if score is None:
                    raise ValueError(f"Missing score: {member_name}, index={idx}")
                score = float(score)
                if not np.isfinite(score):
                    raise ValueError(f"Non-finite score: {member_name}, index={idx}")
                if idx in gpqa_records[meta["model"]]:
                    raise ValueError(f"Duplicate index: {meta['model']}, {idx}")
                gpqa_records[meta["model"]][idx] = score
                gpqa_texts[meta["model"]][idx] = text_value
        except Exception as exc:
            archive_errors.append(f"{member_name}: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}")

if archive_errors:
    fail("GPQA archive errors:\n" + "\n".join(archive_errors[:10]))

# Confirm every candidate has exactly the same 198 prompts in the same order.
first_model = model_names[0]
prompt_order = list(gpqa_records[first_model].keys())
if len(prompt_order) != 198:
    fail(f"Expected 198 GPQA prompts, found {len(prompt_order)} for {first_model}")

for model in model_names:
    if list(gpqa_records[model].keys()) != prompt_order:
        fail(f"Prompt order/index mismatch for GPQA model {model}")
    for idx in prompt_order:
        if gpqa_texts[model][idx] != gpqa_texts[first_model][idx]:
            fail(f"Prompt text mismatch at GPQA index {idx}, model {model}")

# Apply the frozen router; only now are GPQA scores used for evaluation.
gpqa_X = np.array(
    [
        [feature_row(gpqa_texts[first_model][idx])[name] for name in FEATURES]
        for idx in prompt_order
    ],
    dtype=float,
)
if gpqa_X.shape != (198, len(FEATURES)):
    fail(f"Unexpected GPQA feature shape: {gpqa_X.shape}")

predicted = np.column_stack([m.predict(gpqa_X) for m in final_models])
router_choice = predicted.argmax(axis=1)
router_model_names = np.array(model_names)[router_choice]

router_scores = np.array([
    gpqa_records[router_model_names[i]][idx]
    for i, idx in enumerate(prompt_order)
], dtype=float)
global_scores = np.array([
    gpqa_records[global_model][idx] for idx in prompt_order
], dtype=float)

differences = router_scores - global_scores
rng_boot = np.random.default_rng(SEED)
boot_means = np.empty(BOOTSTRAPS)
for b in range(BOOTSTRAPS):
    sample = rng_boot.integers(0, len(differences), len(differences))
    boot_means[b] = differences[sample].mean()
ci_low, ci_high = np.quantile(boot_means, [0.025, 0.975])

wins = int(np.sum(differences > 0))
ties = int(np.sum(differences == 0))
losses = int(np.sum(differences < 0))

with DETAIL.open("w", encoding="utf-8-sig", newline="") as f:
    writer = csv.writer(f)
    writer.writerow([
        "index", "router_model", "router_score",
        "global_model", "global_score", "paired_difference"
    ])
    for i, idx in enumerate(prompt_order):
        writer.writerow([
            idx, router_model_names[i], router_scores[i],
            global_model, global_scores[i], differences[i]
        ])

with OUT.open("w", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    f.write("DAY 4 — HELD-OUT GPQA TRANSFER EVALUATION\n")
    f.write("=" * 48 + "\n")
    f.write("Protocol: source-only tuning and baseline selection; GPQA held out.\n")
    f.write(f"Source domains: {', '.join(source_domains)}\n")
    f.write(f"Source prompts: {len(rows)}\n")
    f.write(f"Candidate models compared: {len(model_names)}\n")
    f.write(f"GPQA evaluation prompts: {len(prompt_order)}\n")
    f.write(f"Selected alpha: {best_alpha}\n")
    f.write(f"Global baseline model: {global_model}\n\n")
    f.write(f"Router mean GPQA score: {router_scores.mean():.6f}\n")
    f.write(f"Global baseline mean GPQA score: {global_scores.mean():.6f}\n")
    f.write(f"Mean paired difference (router - global): {differences.mean():.6f}\n")
    f.write(f"95% prompt-bootstrap percentile CI: [{ci_low:.6f}, {ci_high:.6f}]\n")
    f.write(f"Prompt wins / ties / losses: {wins} / {ties} / {losses}\n")
    f.write(f"Bootstrap resamples: {BOOTSTRAPS}; seed: {SEED}\n\n")
    f.write("Interpretation guardrails:\n")
    f.write("- This estimates performance on this GPQA sample only.\n")
    f.write("- A confidence interval including zero does not establish improvement.\n")
    f.write("- The prompt bootstrap treats prompts as resampling units.\n")
    f.write("- No broad-domain generalization or novelty claim follows from this result.\n")
    f.write("- Model scores are benchmark-provided scores; scoring semantics still matter.\n")
    f.write(f"\nPer-prompt output: {DETAIL}\n")
    f.write(f"Source-only config: {CONFIG}\n")

print("DAY 4 GPQA TRANSFER RUN COMPLETE")

def load_feature_function(path):
    tree = ast.parse(path.read_text(encoding="utf-8-sig"))
    node = next(
        n for n in tree.body
        if isinstance(n, (ast.FunctionDef, ast.AsyncFunctionDef))
        and n.name == "feature_row"
    )
    namespace = {"re": re, "math": math}
    exec(compile(ast.Module(body=[node], type_ignores=[]),
                 str(path), "exec"), namespace)
    return namespace["feature_row"]


def get_text(rec):
    q = rec.get("origin_query")
    if isinstance(q, str) and q.strip():
        return q
    return rec.get("prompt")


def macro_utility(scores, choices, domains):
    chosen = scores[np.arange(len(choices)), choices]
    return float(np.mean([
        chosen[np.asarray(domains) == d].mean() for d in DOMAINS
    ]))


def fit_models(X, Y, alpha, indices):
    fitted = []
    for j in range(Y.shape[1]):
        model = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), Ridge(alpha=alpha))
        model.fit(X[indices], Y[indices, j])
        fitted.append(model)
    return fitted


def bootstrap_ci(differences, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n = len(differences)
    values = np.empty(BOOTSTRAPS)
    for start in range(0, BOOTSTRAPS, 100):
        end = min(start + 100, BOOTSTRAPS)
        sample = rng.integers(0, n, size=(end-start, n))
        values[start:end] = differences[sample].mean(axis=1)
    return np.quantile(values, [0.025, 0.975])

# Research 3 � Consolidated Research Assessment

Date: 2026-10-23

## Decision

CONDITIONAL GO for an empirical study of cross-benchmark routing transfer.
NO-GO for claiming a novel routing algorithm or superiority over the strongest
fixed-model baseline on the evidence currently available.

## Research question

How reliably does a lightweight lexical-feature router transfer across
benchmark families, and which error patterns explain its performance gap
relative to the strongest fixed-model baseline?

This question is provisional. Novelty is not established until the closest
prior work and supplementary materials have been reviewed in full.

## Method

- Source data: 750 ArenaHard prompts across coding, creative writing, and math.
- Features: 11 lexical and structural prompt features.
- Router: Ridge regression predicting per-model scores.
- Source tuning: source-domain validation; selected regularization varies
  across repeated validation splits.
- Target evaluations: GPQA and MMLU-Pro test_3000.
- Candidate pool: Day 4 uses 33 models; Days 5�8 use 13.
- Repeated splits: seeds 2026�2035; target prompts are reused across seeds.

## Main results

### MMLU-Pro

- Router accuracy: 83.8667%.
- Gemini 2.5 Pro: 85.6667%.
- Difference: -1.80 percentage points.
- Paired prompt-bootstrap 95% CI: [-2.50, -1.133] percentage points
  in the Day 5 report's rounded values.
- The router trails Gemini across all ten Day 7 source-validation splits.

### GPQA

- Day 4 router: 81.8182%.
- Gemini 2.5 Pro: 84.8485%.
- Day 4 difference: -3.0303 percentage points.
- Day 4 paired prompt-bootstrap 95% CI:
  [-7.0707, +1.0101] percentage points.
- Across Day 8 source-validation splits, the router trails Gemini by
  approximately 2.53�3.03 percentage points.

### Baseline sensitivity

The apparent advantage changes with the source-validation-selected
baseline. The positive Day 6 comparison is against Qwen3 Thinking, not
Gemini 2.5 Pro. It does not establish superiority over the strongest
fixed-model reference.

The repeated seeds share the same target prompts and are not independent
target replications.

## Permitted conclusions

- The evaluated lexical-feature Ridge router underperformed Gemini 2.5 Pro
  on the two held-out benchmarks reported here.
- Apparent gains over a source-validation-selected baseline depend on
  which baseline is selected.
- The current evidence does not establish reliable superiority, broad
  cross-benchmark generalization, or a novel routing contribution.
- The results may motivate a careful empirical study of routing failure
  under benchmark-family shift.

## Limitations and unresolved work

1. Complete direct review of the closest papers and supplementary materials.
2. Resolve archive provenance, dataset terms, licenses, and redistribution rights.
3. Verify the semantics of archived scores and keep cost/latency claims excluded
   until aggregation and units are validated.
4. Audit source selection, feature construction, prompt alignment, and target
   evaluation paths across all scripts.
5. Recompute paired results from the aligned per-prompt score matrices where
   needed and document the analysis protocol.
6. Define any subgroup or error analysis before inspecting subgroup outcomes;
   treat exploratory analyses as descriptive.
7. Compare against published routing baselines only where a fair and reproducible
   comparison is feasible.

## Reproducibility note

The Day 4 feature-column ordering was hardened after the saved Day 4 run.
The corrected script passed Python syntax validation, but the experiment was
not rerun. The saved Day 4 results therefore remain the output of the original
execution. The current extractor's feature order matches the explicit FEATURES
list, so the change is defensive rather than evidence of a known result error.

The Day 8 script heading was corrected after the summary artifact was generated.
The summary heading correction is documentation-only and does not change results.

## Final recommendation

Do not add another experiment until the provenance and prior-art blockers
are resolved and a specific, preregisterable analysis is defined. If those
checks reveal no defensible gap, retain this work as a transparent negative
result and research-portfolio artifact rather than claiming a novel method.
